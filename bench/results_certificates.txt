NOTES

  "proven optimal exactly" requires the primal point to be EXACTLY feasible in
  rational arithmetic.  A point produced in floating point almost never is, so
  one instance out of fifty-five is the expected number, not a disappointment.

  "rigorous bound established" is the useful verdict: L(y) is a valid lower
  bound on the optimum whatever the primal point turns out to be, and the exact
  distance from it to the reported objective is printed.  On most instances that
  distance is at the 1e-10 level or below.

  scsd1 and scsd6 are the two that do not support their claim.  Both have small
  objectives (8.67 and 50.5) and reduced costs converged to about 2e-9 rather
  than to rounding level, so the safe-bounding penalty lands just over the 1e-6
  relative threshold.  That is a real, if narrow, statement about how far the
  duals converged on those two models -- it is not hidden in the totals.

  Certificates are not yet produced for two paths: the branch-and-bound tree
  emits no dual multipliers, and infeasibility detected inside presolve produces
  no Farkas ray.  Both are reported by the checker as unprovable rather than
  passed silently.
